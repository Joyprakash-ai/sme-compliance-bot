# General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) - 2016/679/EU

**Effective Date:** 25 May 2018
**Jurisdiction:** European Union
**Directive:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679

## Overview
The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a comprehensive data protection law that applies to all organizations processing personal data of individuals in the European Union, regardless of where the organization is located.

## Key Principles (Article 5)
1. **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency:** Processing must be lawful, fair, and transparent to the data subject.
2. **Purpose limitation:** Collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes.
3. **Data minimization:** Adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary.
4. **Accuracy:** Accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date.
5. **Storage limitation:** Kept in identifiable form no longer than necessary.
6. **Integrity and confidentiality:** Processed securely.
7. **Accountability:** Controller responsible for compliance.

## Data Subject Rights (Articles 12-23)
- **Right to access** (Article 15): Individuals can request access to their data.
- **Right to rectification** (Article 16): Individuals can have inaccurate data corrected.
- **Right to erasure** (Article 17): "Right to be forgotten" under certain circumstances.
- **Right to restrict processing** (Article 18): Individuals can request limitation of processing.
- **Right to data portability** (Article 20): Receive personal data in structured format.
- **Right to object** (Article 21): Object to processing based on legitimate interests.
- **Rights related to automated decision-making** (Article 22): Right to human intervention.

## Lawful Bases for Processing (Article 6)
Processing is lawful only if at least one of the following applies:
1. Consent of the data subject
2. Performance of a contract
3. Compliance with a legal obligation
4. Protection of vital interests
5. Performance of a task in the public interest
6. Legitimate interests pursued by the controller

## Requirements for Organizations
- **Data Protection Officer** (Article 37): Required for public authorities, large-scale monitoring, or special categories of data.
- **Records of Processing Activities** (Article 30): Documentation of processing activities.
- **Data Protection Impact Assessment** (Article 35): Required for high-risk processing.
- **Data Breach Notification** (Article 33): Notify supervisory authority within 72 hours.
- **Privacy by Design and Default** (Article 25): Build data protection into systems.

## Penalties (Article 83)
- **Lower tier:** Up to €10 million or 2% of global annual turnover.
- **Upper tier:** Up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover.

## SME Considerations
- **Threshold:** Applies to all organizations regardless of size.
- **Exceptions:** Some obligations are relaxed for SMEs with fewer than 250 employees.
- **Documentation:** SMEs may not need to maintain records of processing activities unless processing is regular, likely to result in risk, or involves special categories of data.

## Practical Compliance Steps for SMEs
1. **Data Mapping:** Understand what personal data you collect and process.
2. **Privacy Notice:** Update privacy policies to be GDPR-compliant.
3. **Consent Management:** Implement proper consent mechanisms.
4. **Security Measures:** Implement appropriate technical and organizational measures.
5. **Data Processing Agreements:** Have contracts with third-party processors.
6. **Breach Response Plan:** Prepare procedures for data breach notification.
